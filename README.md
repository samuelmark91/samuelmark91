# Customer Churn Prediction & Retention Prioritization

**An R analysis by Mark Samuel** comparing customer churn models and translating predicted risk into a hypothetical outreach list. Developed from my customer churn coursework using artificial telecom data.

## Question and result

Can account and usage information identify likely churners, and how many would a capacity-limited contact list capture?

A random forest was selected using training cross-validation. In a held-out sample of **999 records**, its highest-risk **200 records captured 118 of 141 observed churners (83.7%)**, with **59.0% precision** and **4.18× lift** over the test churn rate. These are retrospective ranking results on an artificial dataset, not customers saved or a measured retention effect.

![Precision and recall at 20 percent contact capacity](outputs/03_contact_capacity.png)

## Data and design

- Source: `modeldata::mlc_churn`, 5,000 artificial records with a yes/no churn outcome.
- Stratified split: **4,001 training records** and **999 test records**. Training has 566 churners; test has 141.
- Thirteen declared account/usage predictors; geographic codes and usage-charge fields are excluded to keep the demonstration compact and reduce redundant inputs.
- Logistic regression, CART decision tree and random forest use identical five-fold training resamples.
- Tree complexity and forest mtry are tuned within training CV. The model is chosen by mean CV ROC AUC before making test predictions.
- `churn = yes` is always positive. Probability columns are accessed by name.
- The classification cutoff **0.5** and contact capacity **20%** are fixed in advance.
- The baseline assigns every record the training churn prevalence as its probability.

## Held-out findings

| Model | Training CV ROC AUC | Test ROC AUC | Test average precision | Precision at 0.5 | Recall at 0.5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic regression | 0.823 | 0.819 | 0.525 | 67.4% | 22.0% |
| Decision tree | 0.904 | 0.888 | 0.772 | 88.5% | 70.9% |
| **Random forest — selected by CV** | **0.926** | **0.888** | **0.846** | **92.8%** | **73.0%** |
| Constant-probability baseline | — | 0.500 | 0.141 | Undefined | 0.0% |

At the 0.5 cutoff, random forest identifies **103 churners**, misses **38**, and incorrectly flags **8 non-churners**. Its accuracy is **95.4%**. The baseline's **85.9% accuracy** identifies zero churners, illustrating why accuracy alone is inadequate.

The decision tree and forest have nearly identical test ROC AUC. The forest was selected from training CV, rather than by choosing whichever test metric looked best. The difference between its CV and test AUC also shows why the held-out assessment matters.

![Held-out ROC curves](outputs/02_test_roc.png)

## Capacity changes the operating point

| Random forest policy | Selected records | Observed churners captured | Precision | Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Probability at least 0.5 | 111 | 103 | 92.8% | 73.0% |
| Highest-risk 20% (rounded up) | 200 | 118 | 59.0% | 83.7% |

The broader list captures more churners and includes **82 non-churners**. That tradeoff can inform a future outreach-capacity discussion. Actual contact costs, offer response and treatment effects are absent, so no ROI is calculated. The 200-record list is 20.02% of the 999 test rows because capacity is rounded up.

Lift compares list precision with the **14.1% test churn rate**. For the constant baseline, equal scores are ordered by source row; its observed contact-list result is an arbitrary tied ordering. Expected random-selection lift is 1.

## Interpretation

The fitted forest ranks daytime usage, customer-service calls and the international-plan indicator highly. Importance and training segment differences describe predictive associations. They do not prove why people leave or which intervention would prevent churn.

## Reproduce in RStudio

1. Extract this folder and open `CustomerChurn.Rproj`.
2. In the Console run:

```r
source("00_setup.R")
source("01_run_analysis.R")
```

3. After `Finished` appears, open:

```r
browseURL(normalizePath("outputs/report.html"))
```

The project includes the actual report, five PNG charts, CSV outputs, source fingerprint, split manifest and run/session records from the completed **November 13, 2026** run. Model training may take several minutes. Package setup needs internet access. Seeds and fold indices are saved when rerunning; version differences may change results.

Large generated model and source-snapshot RDS files are omitted from this publication package and regenerated by the script. The original full submitted archive is unchanged. The recorded fingerprint refers to the source snapshot produced during the verified run.

## Improvements from the assignments

I replaced a lasso input matrix containing the outcome with explicit response-excluding formulas; corrected probability-label handling; compared models under a consistent validation design; and added baseline, precision-recall and contact-capacity reporting. The first published version focuses on three models. Original assignments and unrelated YouTube credentials are not bundled.

## Limits

The source is artificial and provides no observation dates, prediction horizon or intervention outcomes. A random holdout cannot establish future performance. Calibration, uncertainty and temporal validation need further work. CV scores used for selection can be optimistic. Do not keep tuning after seeing the test results.
